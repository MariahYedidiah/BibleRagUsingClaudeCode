# In the beginning was the Word

The question quotes the opening words of the Gospel of John [JHN.1.1]. Both the WEB and the New Testament Greek (SBLGNT) were retrieved for it, and they agree: the verse makes three statements about "the Word" (Greek *ho logos*). The Word was in the beginning, the Word was with God, and the Word was God. The next verse repeats the point that this Word was "in the beginning with God" [JHN.1.2].

Later in the same prologue the passage says what happened to the Word: it "became flesh" and lived among people, and its glory was seen, "full of grace and truth" [JHN.1.14]. The machine translation of the SBLGNT renders the verb as "dwelt". The specialist noted that the Greek verb (*eskēnōsen*) may recall tabernacle language. That is only a suggestion about the word; the dataset doesn't link this verse to the tabernacle.

Two other New Testament passages use the same language. The opening of 1 John speaks of what "was from the beginning", which the writers heard, saw and touched, "concerning the Word of life" [1JN.1.1]. In Revelation, the name of the rider "is called The Word of God" [REV.19.13]. In the SBLGNT text of this verse, two words are marked as places where manuscripts differ (*bebammenon* "dipped" and *keklētai* "is called").

**Old Testament background.** John 1:1–14 has no quotation formula. The links below are verified cross-references in the OpenBible dataset (many votes). They show the background readers have connected with these verses. They don't mean John claims to quote these texts.

- John's opening "in the beginning" goes back to the first verse of Genesis [GEN.1.1]. In the Septuagint Greek (Swete LXX) the wording is the same as in John 1:1 (*en archē*). The Septuagint has "made" (*epoiēsen*) where the Hebrew text (OSHB/WLC) has "created" (*bara*). The machine translation of the Hebrew notes that the verse can also be read "When God began to create…". Genesis does not mention a "word" in this verse.
- John 1:1 is also linked to Wisdom speaking in Proverbs, "in the beginning of his work, before his deeds of old" [PRO.8.22]. Here the sources differ, and the difference should be kept. The WEB has "The LORD possessed me". The machine translation of the Hebrew has "acquired me" and notes that the Hebrew verb can mean "acquired", "possessed" or "created". The Septuagint has "created me" (*ektisen*), "as the beginning of his ways".
- John 1:14 ("we saw his glory") is linked to the promise that "the LORD's glory shall be revealed, and all flesh shall see it together" [ISA.40.5]. Here the Septuagint differs from the Hebrew text: it says all flesh will see "the salvation of God". It also has "the Lord has spoken" where the Hebrew has "the mouth of the LORD has spoken".

The dataset has three weaker links from John 1:14, which were not checked further. It possibly alludes to Isaiah 53:2, Isaiah 60:1 and Psalm 45:2. Other links (Isaiah 9:6, Isaiah 7:14, Psalm 45:6, Isaiah 40:9, Psalm 2:7) are thematic similarities only.
