# What does Genesis say about creation?

The retrieved passages all come from the opening creation account of Genesis (chapters 1 and 2) and describe it in four steps.

**God creates everything.** Genesis opens by saying that God created the heavens and the earth "in the beginning" [GEN.1.1]. The Hebrew text (OSHB/WLC) uses the verb ברא with God as its only subject. The machine translation notes that the opening word can also be read as "When God began to create...". The Septuagint Greek (Swete LXX) has a different verb here, ἐποίησεν ("made"), not a verb specific to creating, and it has the singular "heaven" where WEB has "the heavens" (machine translation).

**God creates by speaking.** Light comes into being when God says "Let there be light" [GEN.1.3]. The Hebrew, the Septuagint and WEB agree in sense here. The Septuagint phrases it as "let light come to be" (γίνομαι).

**Living things and humankind.** God makes the land animals and livestock "after their kind" and sees that it is good [GEN.1.25]. Then God says "Let us make" humankind in his image and likeness, to rule over the fish, birds, livestock and the earth [GEN.1.26]. The Hebrew uses a plural cohortive ("let us make"). The Hebrew machine translation lists several readings of this plural (divine council, plural of majesty, deliberation) and doesn't choose between them. The next verse says that God created humankind in his own image, "male and female" [GEN.1.27]. The Hebrew uses ברא three times in this verse. The Septuagint uses ἐποίησεν ("made") all three times, and it says "according to the image of God" where WEB has "in God's image" (machine translation). In Gen 1:26 the Swete text has one word that may be damaged (πάτης, probably for πάσης, "all"), so the machine translation reads it from context.

**Completion and rest.** God blesses the seventh day and makes it holy, because on it he rested from all his work of creation [GEN.2.3]. Literally, the Hebrew of this verse says "that God created to make". The machine translation notes that WEB's "his work of creation which he had done" smooths over this idiom. For Gen 2:2 only the Septuagint was kept. It says that God completed his works **on the sixth day** and rested on the seventh [GEN.2.2]. The Septuagint specialist's comparison notes that WEB, following the Hebrew, has God finishing his work on the *seventh* day. The WEB and Hebrew texts of Gen 2:2 weren't retrieved as passages, so this difference rests on the specialist's comparison. The two readings are reported as they stand, without trying to reconcile them.

The evidence covers only part of the account. It doesn't include Gen 1:2 (the formless earth and the Spirit over the waters), the other days of creation, Gen 1:31 ("very good"), or the second creation narrative of Gen 2:4 onward (the forming of the man, the garden, the woman). For those, these results support no claims.

**Evidence confidence: high**: 15 passages kept; 5 references found in two or more languages; 12 passages found by both vector and lexical search.
