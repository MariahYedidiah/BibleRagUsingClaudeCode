# Who is the voice crying in the wilderness?

In the Gospels, the "voice of one crying in the wilderness" is John the Baptist, and the words come from the prophet Isaiah.

**Who the voice is.** In John's Gospel the speaker names himself. When questioned, he answers that he is the voice of one crying in the wilderness, "as Isaiah the prophet said" [JHN.1.23]. The specialists read "he" here as John the Baptist from the surrounding narrative, which is not shown here. In the New Testament Greek (SBLGNT) he says *Ἐγὼ φωνή*, "I am a voice" (machine translation). Matthew says of John, "this is he who was spoken of by Isaiah the prophet" [MAT.3.3]. Mark [MRK.1.3] and Luke [LUK.3.4] quote the same words when they introduce John's ministry, and Luke cites "the book of the words of Isaiah the prophet". In Mark the verse continues a quotation that begins in the previous verse, which is not shown here. So all four Gospels attach the voice to John: John's Gospel through his own claim, the other three by applying the prophecy to him.

**Where the words come from.** All four Gospel verses have a verified cross-reference (OpenBible.info) to Isaiah 40:3 [ISA.40.3]. In Isaiah the voice itself is not named. WEB calls it only "the voice of one who calls out", and the machine translation of the Hebrew renders it "A voice is calling".

**A difference between the texts, not to be smoothed over.** The Hebrew (OSHB/WLC) and the Septuagint Greek (Swete LXX) of Isaiah 40:3 attach "in the wilderness" to different words:
- In the Hebrew and in WEB, "in the wilderness" goes with the command: the road is to be prepared in the wilderness. The Hebrew specialist notes that the Hebrew accents favour this reading, and that the parallel line adds "in the desert" for the highway.
- In the Septuagint, "in the wilderness" goes with the voice: *ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ βοῶντος*, "of one crying in the wilderness". This is the wording the Gospels quote. The Septuagint's second line also differs: "make straight the paths of our God" (machine translation), where WEB has "make a level highway in the desert for our God", and the LXX verse has no "desert".

In the Gospel quotations, the wilderness is where the voice is crying, as in the Septuagint. In the Hebrew it is where the way is to be prepared.

The Greek specialist flagged two smaller points. John's Gospel has "make straight the way of the Lord" (*Εὐθύνατε*), where the others have "prepare". The SBLGNT also marks small text-critical variants in Matthew 3:3 and Luke 3:4.

Other passages the cross-reference data links to these verses (Malachi 3:1 and 4:6, Isaiah 57:14 and 62:10) have only "possible allusion" status, were not retrieved, and are not used here.
