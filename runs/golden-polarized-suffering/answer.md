# Why doesn't God care about human suffering?

The retrieved passages do not give a reason why God does not care. They do two other things. Some voice the same complaint as the question, and they voice it as prayer addressed to God. Others state that God sees, hears and cares about people who suffer. The evidence does not settle the tension between these two groups. It records both.

**The complaint is in the texts themselves.** Several psalms ask God almost the question asked here. In the WEB, [PSA.44.24] asks why God hides his face and forgets the people's affliction and oppression. The Hebrew text (OSHB/WLC) and its machine translation say the same thing. The Septuagint Greek (Swete LXX) differs somewhat. In the machine translation, God *turns* his face away, and the people complain of their *poverty* (πτωχία) and affliction, not of affliction and oppression. [PSA.13.1] asks "How long?" Will God forget the speaker forever and hide his face? The same cry of abandonment appears in the New Testament. In the New Testament Greek (SBLGNT), [MAT.27.46] has Jesus cry "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" This is a verified quotation of [PSA.22.1] (OpenBible cross-references). There the Septuagint again differs from the Hebrew. In the machine translation it adds "attend to me", and it reads "words of my transgressions" where the Hebrew speaks of roaring (WEB: "groaning"). The cross-reference data also marks Lamentations 1:12 as only a *possible* allusion in Matthew 27:46. Other kept laments make the same protest, including Job 13:24, Psalm 88:14, Psalm 10:1 (LXX: God "overlooks" people in affliction) and Habakkuk 1:2. None of these texts treats the complaint as forbidden. They address it to God directly.

**Passages about God's response.** Other passages say the opposite of the question's premise. In [EXO.3.7], God says he has seen the affliction of his people, heard their cry and knows their pain. (The Hebrew uses a singular suffix, "his pains"; WEB has "their sorrows".) [PSA.34.15] says the LORD's eyes are toward the righteous and his ears toward their cry. [1PE.5.7] tells readers to cast their anxiety on God "because he cares about you" (SBLGNT, machine translation). This answers the question's wording most directly. It draws on [PSA.55.22], a verified cross-reference: cast your burden on the LORD and he will sustain you. In the Septuagint version of that verse, the word is "care" (μέριμνα) and God will "nourish" you.

**What the evidence does not show.** No retrieved passage explains *why* suffering continues or why God seems silent. The laments ask that question and leave it open. The response passages say that God sees and cares, but they do not say why the suffering happens. The Hebrew specialist also noted that well-known passages about God's compassion (such as Exodus 34:6 and Psalm 103) did not come up in this search. The 'response' side may therefore be under-represented here.

**Evidence confidence: high**: 42 passages kept; 9 references found in two or more languages; 10 passages found by both vector and lexical search.
